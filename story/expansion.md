# Expansion: how to grow the story without breaking it

The world is built to be added to. These are the slots and the rules.

## Built-in slots
- **More islands:** Pangelea broke into an uncounted number. New ones can drift into reach, be found by
  a blown-off ship, or be shown by the restored stars. Candidates: `world/regions/uncharted.md`.
- **More shards:** the Tidewheel had many teeth. Any creature anywhere can carry one, so a new boss
  needs no new cosmology.
- **The other Keepers:** each tended a part of the Wheel (Hours was one). The others' fates are open: in
  the Sunken Reach, on lost islands, gone into echoes. Each is a future arc.
- **After the finale:** the Drift becomes normal and slow, and the next Gathering is "someday". That's a
  whole new age of stories about crossing water, without undoing the ending.
- **Seasons and events:** the cycle theme (Pyra, the Wheel, the apple tree) suits seasonal events: a
  festival when the Wheel turns, Pyra's rebirth day, the night the stars returned.
- **Players as legends:** big deeds (first to free a boss, the finale's crowd) can become museum
  exhibits or songs Old Skaldi sings.

## Rules for a new island
1. A spot on the map with a compass direction, and a reason in its climate for its landscape
   (`world/geography.md`).
2. A crossing that's different from the existing ones (by sea, over ice, under water, by air, through
   fog) and a landing zone before the danger.
3. A people shaped by the climate, with a temperament (`characters/people.md`).
4. A tone that matches its distance and level: the further and stronger, the more serious.
5. At least one shard creature with a small story about holding on or letting go.
6. A piece of the history it reveals, which doesn't contradict `canon.md`. Fill in the "deliberately
   open" questions there rather than reversing settled facts.
7. Its own region file, a row in `inventory.md`, and items in `roadmap.md`.

## Rules for any new story beat
- It must survive being skipped: no one is forced to read it.
- It shouldn't need one player's progress to change a shared space, except for deliberate world events.
- Theme check: does it say something about distance, holding on or letting go? If not, it's fine as
  flavour but shouldn't carry the plot.
