# Pitch: The Drift

## Premise (one paragraph)
Long ago the slimes lived on one land, **Pangelea**, which breathed like a tide. Over ages it drew
together (a **Gathering**) and slowly spread apart (a **Drift**), turned by a great clockwork called the
**Tidewheel** that an order of **Keepers** tended. At the end of the last Gathering, the Keeper of Hours
could not bear the coming Drift and tried to stop the Wheel. It jammed instead. Pangelea broke into
islands that have been drifting apart ever since, *only outward, with no return*. The moment it broke is
called **the Stopped Hour**. Today the sea between the islands is widening, and the drift is speeding
up. The slimes of Slimeville, on the quiet home island of **Greenhollow**, have nearly forgotten the
other islands exist.

## What the players do
Leave home, cross the widening **Glass Sea**, and reconnect the scattered islands: free each island's
**Anchor** (a shard of the Tidewheel that fused with a creature and drove it mad), relight the old routes,
and finally climb to the **Aether Citadel**, torn loose into the sky, where the Keeper of Hours, now
**Chronos**, is trying to finish what he began and stop time entirely, freezing everything before it
drifts out of reach.

## Theme
**Distance isn't the end of closeness.** This is a game a group of friends plays together, with voices
that carry, chat that reaches offline friends, and houses you invite each other into. The story is about
the same thing: a world pulling apart, and people choosing to stay connected anyway. Chronos is the dark
mirror of that. He loves the world so much that he would freeze it rather than let anything leave.

The ending is not "pull the islands back together by force". The players restart the Tidewheel. That
means accepting the Drift, trusting that a Gathering will come again in its own time (generations away),
and building crossings in the meantime. Bittersweet, earned, and grown-up.

## Tone: it hardens with distance
- **Home (Greenhollow, Wildwood):** cozy, warm, storybook. Puns are fine. Danger is light.
- **North (Frostpeak):** quiet and melancholy. Stoic people and the first real hints.
- **South (Emberdeep):** harsh, industrial and angry. Grief and the truth about the Stopped Hour.
- **Sky (the Citadel):** eerie, solemn, epic. Almost nobody left, only echoes.

Players grow up as they travel, and the writing should grow up with them (`delivery.md`).

## Why the geography matters
At the moment every stage sits behind the next one's back door, so the world feels like four rooms in a
row. In the story the islands are **far apart and getting further**. Reaching them should feel like a
voyage: different seas, different skies, a different way to cross each time (`world/geography.md`).
