# Geography: the Glass Sea

## The problem this fixes
Today the stages are a chain of back doors: village → Wildwood → Highlands → Caldera → Citadel, each one
a short walk from the last. Snow sits next to a volcano, which sits next to a sky city. Nothing feels far
away, and nothing explains why the land changes.

- **Greenhollow** (west): home. Temperate, green, sheltered. The only island that still feels safe.
- **Frostpeak** (far north): fjords, tundra, glacier, a star-crater on the summit.
- **Emberdeep** (far south): a volcano island. Black sand, ash plains, hot springs, a lava heart.
- **The Aether Citadel** (above): the Keepers' city, torn into the sky. It circles over the Maelstrom.
- **The Maelstrom** (centre): where Pangelea's heart sank. Endgame and post-game. `[proposed]`
- **The Sunken Reach** (under the centre): drowned Pangelean ruins on the seabed. `[proposed]`
- **Lantern Fen** (east): a marsh of ghost lights, an optional side island. `[proposed]`

## Room to grow
Pangelea broke into **many** islands. The map above shows only the ones Slimeville's charts know. The
Glass Sea has no edge on the map. New islands can be "drifting into reach", "found by a ship blown off
course" or "shown by the restored stars" at any time, and none of these contradicts canon. The compass
points are not full: east, the far west and the deep are all open. Rules for slotting in a new island are
in `../expansion.md`.

## Climate logic (every place obeys it)
- North is cold and south is hot. Up is thin air, wind and starlight. Down is dark, pressure and ruins.
- An island's landscape layers from its **shore** (where you arrive) to its **interior** and then its
  **height or heart**. Each stage is a walk inland and upward, not a strip of random biomes.
- Each island has a landmark you can see from far away: Frostpeak's aurora, Emberdeep's smoke column, the
  Citadel as a pale shape in the sky. **Every outdoor sky draws the others in their compass direction**,
  small and far off. That's what makes the world feel big.

## Distance and travel: a different crossing every time
| route | how | feel |
|---|---|---|
| Slimeville → Wildwood | on foot, through the east gate | familiar, the same island |
| Greenhollow → Frostpeak | **the ferry** from Greenhollow Harbour: a ship's deck room crossing a cold sea | the first time you leave home |
| Frostpeak → Emberdeep | a ship through the **Steam Straits**, steered by the stars (needs the Astral Wyrm beaten) | the long voyage |
| Emberdeep → Citadel | a **balloon** riding the volcano's thermal column up through the clouds | the climb into the sky |
| any harbour → any harbour | once a route is opened, sailing back is quick | the world gets connected again |

- **Crossings are rooms, not loading screens.** A deck that moves through a scrolling sea and sky for
  roughly a minute: friends hang out, talk, fish over the rail, fight things that climb aboard. It's the
  game's natural social space, and it makes distance something you *feel*.
- Back doors between stages go away. Each island is reached from its **harbour or landing**, and the
  harbour is the stage's first zone.
- A quick way home is fine once a route is open (sailing back is short, and a Return scroll still works).
  The first crossing should be the slow one.

## The drift as a live feature `[proposed]`
The sea really does widen: distant islands in each skybox shrink a little each real week, and crossing
art shows more open water. Restarting the Tidewheel (the end of the main arc) stops this, and that change
is visible in every sky. It's cheap to do (sky-drawing parameters) and very strong for the story.
